Guest speakers · 6 takeaways

The advice I remembered was the advice I could use

These conversations covered engineering, college, communication, and career decisions. I kept the lessons that changed the way I approached a project, presentation, or next step.

01

Altec engineering

The Altec conversation helped me see that an engineering idea has to answer a real customer or community need. I carried that lesson into the AquaTec challenge, where I researched water filtration before deciding what the design should include.

02

Virginia Tech researchers

Dr. Barry Miller walked us through the Helmet Lab’s testing process and explained how impact data becomes a safety decision. Dr. Ikeda Gilbert introduced hydroelasticity and showed how a towing tank can recreate the forces acting on a structure in water.

03

MATLAB with Juan

This session connected code to the kind of work engineers actually do. Instead of seeing MATLAB as another programming tool, I saw how it could repeat calculations, organize data, and make a pattern easier to understand through a graph.

04

Toastmasters and communication

Debate taught me how to speak with confidence. Toastmasters made me think more carefully about pace, eye contact, structure, and whether the audience could follow me. That difference has helped me in presentations and interviews.

05

Career preparation workshops

The résumé and interview workshops taught me to stop hiding behind broad descriptions. Saying that I joined a project is not enough. I need to explain what I did, what changed because of it, and what I learned.

06

Allison Ward’s engineering path

Allison Ward, a former Burton student, spoke honestly about Georgia Tech, internships, Disney, networking, and her move from mechanical to electrical engineering. Her story made it clear that changing direction can be part of finding the right work, not a sign that the first plan failed.

I kept the photos, worksheets, and handwritten notes because they show what I was doing in the room, not just the polished summary I wrote later.
