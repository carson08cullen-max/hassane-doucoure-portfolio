Medical enrichment · Hands-on learning

Learning medicine by seeing, practicing, and asking why.

At the Edward Via College of Osteopathic Medicine, I explored anatomy, clinical skills, and medical technology through hands-on stations. Each activity gave me a closer look at how scientific knowledge becomes patient care.

VCOM experience

The program let me move between the laboratory and simulated clinical settings. I examined tissue, worked with anatomy specimens, practiced suturing and airway management, observed ultrasound imaging, and rehearsed an emergency response. Together, these experiences strengthened my interest in biomedical engineering and accessible healthcare.

Clinical simulation

Airway management practice

I practiced positioning equipment and working carefully with an airway simulator. The station showed me how technique, communication, and reliable tools all matter during time-sensitive care.

Microscopy · 16 seconds

Histology under the microscope

I examined a prepared tissue sample and connected microscopic structures to the organs and systems they support.

Anatomy

Brain anatomy lab

Working with a specimen made the organization of the brain more concrete than a diagram and helped me understand the importance of careful anatomical observation.

Clinical skills

Suturing technique

I practiced placing and spacing sutures on a simulation pad, focusing on control, consistency, and protecting the tissue around a wound.

Airway care · 30 seconds

Intubation simulation

This station introduced the sequence and precision involved in securing an airway and reinforced how medical-device design affects speed, visibility, and safety.

Medical imaging

Ultrasound observation

I watched real-time ultrasound imaging and saw how clinicians translate changing visual information into an understanding of anatomy and function.

Emergency response

CPR training

Using a full-body simulator, I practiced the fundamentals of responding quickly and working through an emergency-care sequence.

What I carried forward: Good healthcare depends on people who can connect biology, engineering, clear communication, and empathy. This experience made me want to keep learning at that intersection.
