Workplace readiness

Doing good work also means being able to explain it

Mock interviews, résumé workshops, presentations, and team projects helped me become more direct, listen better, and follow through when other people were counting on me.

Mock interview

Get to the point

I prepared examples from engineering, debate, athletics, and volunteering. The biggest lesson was simple: a strong answer should not take a long route to reach the point. I practiced naming the situation, saying what I personally did, and ending with the result.

Preparation also made me sound more like myself. I was not trying to remember a script; I knew the experience well enough to explain it naturally.

Résumé workshop

Show the contribution

“Worked on laptops” does not tell an employer much. “Helped source 18 laptops, plan repairs, and deliver the first three devices” shows the scale and my role. Learning that difference changed both my résumé and this portfolio.

Now I try to include the problem, my action, and the result instead of relying on a title to explain the work for me.

Professional communication

Know who you are talking to

Debate taught me to speak under pressure, but workplace communication has a different purpose. A teammate may need the technical details, a supervisor may need the result, and a community partner may need plain language. The facts stay the same, but the explanation has to fit the audience.

Teamwork

Be the person others can rely on

Internships, projects, sports, and volunteering taught me to speak up early when there is a scheduling problem, finish my part, ask useful questions, and take correction without making it personal. Most professionalism shows up in small habits long before a major accomplishment.
